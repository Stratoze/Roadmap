# Roadmap
>
> Long-game classical roadmap. Goal: play Liszt well in 15 to 20 years — and keep going after.
> This is not limited to RCM or ABRSM. Use exam boards only as calibration.
> Priority: public domain, standard editions, easy-to-find high-quality scores and recordings.
> Design principle: every stage develops the four pillars simultaneously —
> Reading, Technique, Ear/Functional, and Musicianship. None is optional.
>
> **Technique is the Movement layer.** Every stage has a **Movement Gate**: the
> [movement archetypes](Movement.md) (M1–M12) you must own before advancing.
> Repertoire is the material you use to train them. The archetypes, not the pieces,
> are what transfer to anything you ever want to play.

## Core principles
- Public domain first when an equally good piece exists.
- Prefer standard urtext editions: Henle, Bärenreiter, Wiener Urtext, ABRSM, Schirmer, Peters, Durand, Editio Musica Budapest.
- Prefer pieces with many recordings and many editions.
- Avoid obscure contest pieces, weak arrangements, and hard-to-find living-composer solos unless they teach something unique.
- Every stage needs:
  - one Baroque or contrapuntal work
  - one Classical sonata or sonatina
  - one Romantic or expressive piece
  - one modern or color piece
  - one etude chosen by technical objective
  - technical work: scales, arpeggios, chords, octaves as appropriate
- Every stage also requires active development of:
  - sight-reading (2 levels below current repertoire)
  - ear training and functional playing
  - **movement acquisition** (the stage's Movement Gate)
  - mental practice and score study away from the piano

## Movement integration by stage
Movement gates replace and extend the old biomechanical focus list. Each gate item is
"owned" when it passes the [Movement diagnostic](Movement.md#movement-diagnostics)
cleanly in repertoire, not just in drills.

| Stage | Movement gate detail | Health note |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0–1 | M1: knuckle-initiated stroke, neutral wrist. M2: arm drop with release. Bench/alignment setup. | Establish P3 neutral wrist as default before speed ever enters. |
| 2–3 | M3: thumb travel without wrist twist. M4: rotation, never stretch. M6: pre-shaped triads. | Stretch-vs-rotation habit set here lasts forever; get it right now. |
| 3–4 | M4 fluency + in/out. M5: small leaps with pre-shape. M8: finger trill → rotation awareness. M10: hand-off legato. M2 pedaled tone. | Eyes-closed leap drill starts here. |
| 4–5 | M11: weight steering for voicing. M5: moderate leaps, eyes-free. M9: substitution. M12: syncopized pedal. | Voicing must be a movement, not an afterthought. |
| 5–6 | M7: octave frame + wrist spring. M5: large leaps. M11 advanced. M4 at velocity. | Octave wrist spring is THE injury line; never cross into arm-hammering. |
| 6–7 | M7 stamina, M8 tremolo, M6 large chords with voicing, M12 half-pedal. | Release budget (P6) now determines endurance. |
| 7–8 | Velocity composition: all archetypes at small amplitude, proximal drive, gait transitions installed. | Self-monitor for micro-tension in long works. |
| 8–9 | La Campanella gate + Liszt gateway. Personal calibration documented. | Recovery protocols become part of practice. |

## Stage 0: Foundations
### Technical goals
- posture, bench height, relaxed hand
- steady pulse
- five-finger patterns
- one-octave scales
- simple triads
- non-legato and basic legato
### Movement gate
- **M1 intro:** knuckle-initiated five-finger stroke; neutral wrist; no pressing after key-bottom.
- **M2:** arm drop onto single notes with immediate release.
- **P1–P3 felt, not just read:** proximal source, dynamic arch, neutral wrist.
- Gate test: play five-finger patterns with visible knuckle initiation and zero wrist dipping.
### Etudes and studies
- one adult method book for structure, optional
- Beyer Op.101 selected
- Lemoine Op.37 very easy selected
- Czerny Op.599 very selected
### Repertoire candidates
- Anonymous minuets from Notebook for Anna Magdalena
- L. Mozart Minuet in F
- Reinagle Allegretto in C
- Hook Minuetto in C
- Türk Arioso in F
- Türk High Spirits
- Beethoven Ukrainian Folk Song
- Schumann very easy Album pieces
- Tchaikovsky very easy Album pieces
### Sight-reading focus
- Note recognition in treble and bass clef
- Five-finger position reading
- Simple rhythms: whole, half, quarter notes
- Keep a steady pulse, do not stop
### Ear/Functional focus
- Sing simple melodies before playing
- Identify major vs minor by ear
- Find notes on the keyboard by interval (steps and skips)
- Play a simple melody with one chord per bar
### Milestone
- play two short pieces with steady pulse
- play one scale hands separately
- keep a relaxed hand with neutral wrist (M1/M2 gate)
- sight-read a five-finger pattern without stopping
- play a simple melody by ear in one key

## When to move to next stage
Move when you can:
- play one piece from each main category with control
- perform a 10 to 30 minute mock recital depending on stage
- recover from slips without stopping
- play technical requirements from memory with even tone
- sight-read material two stages below current level
- describe form and harmony in simple words
- **pass the stage's Movement Gate in repertoire (not just drills)**
- meet the ear/functional milestone for the current stage
Do not move just because time passed.
