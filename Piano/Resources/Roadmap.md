# Roadmap
>
> Long-game classical roadmap. Goal: play Liszt well in 15 to 20 years.
> This is not limited to RCM or ABRSM. Use exam boards only as calibration.
> Priority: public domain, standard editions, easy-to-find high-quality scores and recordings.
> Design principle: every stage develops the four pillars simultaneously —
> Reading, Technique, Ear/Functional, and Musicianship. None is optional.

## Core principles

- Public domain first when an equally good piece exists.
- Prefer standard urtext editions: Henle, Bärenreiter, Wiener Urtext, ABRSM, Schirmer, Peters, Durand, Editio Musica Budapest.
- Prefer pieces with many recordings and many editions.
- Avoid obscure contest pieces, weak arrangements, and hard-to-find living-composer solos unless they teach something unique.
- Every stage needs:
  - one Baroque or contrapuntal work
  - one Classical sonata or sonatina
  - one Romantic or expressive piece
  - one modern or color piece
  - one etude chosen by technical objective
  - technical work: scales, arpeggios, chords, octaves as appropriate
- Every stage also requires active development of:
  - sight-reading (2 levels below current repertoire)
  - ear training and functional playing
  - biomechanical awareness and injury prevention
  - mental practice and score study away from the piano

## Biomechanical integration by stage

Every stage includes biomechanical awareness. This is not optional.

| Stage | Biomechanical focus |
| ---: | --- |
| 0–1 | Neutral wrist position. Whole-arm alignment. No ulnar deviation. Relaxed shoulder. Bench setup. |
| 2–3 | Efficient kinetic chain: forearm → wrist → fingertip. Economy of motion. Begin finger independence. |
| 4–5 | Controlled arm weight. Rotation for arpeggios. Avoid excessive finger lifting. |
| 5–6 | Octave mechanics: wrist as shock absorber. Leaps: prepare in the air, arrive relaxed. |
| 6–7 | Large chord voicing without tension. Stamina through efficiency, not force. |
| 7–8 | Velocity through minimal motion. Whole-body coordination for fortissimo. |
| 8–9 | Sustained virtuosity. Self-monitoring for micro-tension. Recovery protocols. |

## Stage 0: Foundations

### Technical goals

- posture, bench height, relaxed hand
- steady pulse
- five-finger patterns
- one-octave scales
- simple triads
- non-legato and basic legato

### Biomechanical goals

- Establish neutral wrist: forearm, wrist, and fingertip in a straight line
- Recognize ulnar deviation (wrist bending toward pinky) and correct it
- Shoulder relaxed, not elevated
- Whole-arm movement, not finger isolation

### Etudes and studies

- one adult method book for structure, optional
- Beyer Op.101 selected
- Lemoine Op.37 very easy selected
- Czerny Op.599 very selected

### Repertoire candidates

- Anonymous minuets from Notebook for Anna Magdalena
- L. Mozart Minuet in F
- Reinagle Allegretto in C
- Hook Minuetto in C
- Türk Arioso in F
- Türk High Spirits
- Beethoven Ukrainian Folk Song
- Schumann very easy Album pieces
- Tchaikovsky very easy Album pieces

### Sight-reading focus

- Note recognition in treble and bass clef
- Five-finger position reading
- Simple rhythms: whole, half, quarter notes
- Keep a steady pulse, do not stop

### Ear/Functional focus

- Sing simple melodies before playing
- Identify major vs minor by ear
- Find notes on the keyboard by interval (steps and skips)
- Play a simple melody with one chord per bar

### Milestone

- play two short pieces with steady pulse
- play one scale hands separately
- keep a relaxed hand with neutral wrist
- sight-read a five-finger pattern without stopping
- play a simple melody by ear in one key

### Capstone

- 60 to 90 minute recital
- recorded album or public performance
- one major Liszt cycle or sonata
- one work from a different specialization
- real-time sight-reading of professional-level repertoire
- perform anime/J-pop by ear at Animenz level with personal arrangements

## Liszt gateway order

1. Consolation No.3 or No.5
2. Liebestraum No.3
3. Sonetto 104 del Petrarca
4. Vallée d'Obermann
5. Three Concert Etudes selected
6. Paganini Etude No.6 theme and variations, then La campanella with teacher
7. Transcendental Etudes by objective
8. Mephisto Waltz No.1
9. Hungarian Rhapsodies selected
10. Sonata in B minor
11. Dante Sonata
12. Années de pèlerinage cycles and paraphrases

## La Campanella technical prerequisites

Before attempting La Campanella, the following must be secure:

- Large right-hand leaps (10ths and beyond) with spatial accuracy
- Rapid chromatic scales in parallel thirds and sixths
- Bell-tone voicing: melody note clearly above accompaniment
- Left-hand octave stability and resonance
- Sustained velocity without tension for 5+ minutes
- Paganini Etude No.6 theme and variations as preparation
- At least two other Liszt showpieces at performance level
- Teacher supervision mandatory

## When to move to next stage

Move when you can:

- play one piece from each main category with control
- perform a 10 to 30 minute mock recital depending on stage
- recover from slips without stopping
- play technical requirements from memory with even tone
- sight-read material two stages below current level
- describe form and harmony in simple words
- demonstrate the biomechanical focus for the current stage
- meet the ear/functional milestone for the current stage

Do not move just because time passed.
