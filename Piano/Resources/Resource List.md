## Identifying an etude's technical objective

Selection by objective only works if you know the objective.

Sources:

- The RCM syllabus lists the pedagogical purpose of each graded etude.
- Individual editions, such as Alfred Masterwork or Henle, usually include a short pedagogical note.
- The composer's own preface, especially Czerny.
- Ask r/piano, Piano World, Piano Street, or a teacher.

A good question:
Which studies in this opus target this technique?

Chopin and Liszt etudes are major concert repertoire, not daily drills.
They demand mature technique and benefit from teacher supervision.

## Polyphonic work

Choose individual pieces at the appropriate grade.
The RCM graded Baroque lists are more reliable than assuming all Two-Part Inventions are equal.

Approximate progression:

- Anna Magdalena Notebook selections
- small preludes
- selected Two-Part Inventions
- Sinfonias
- French or English Suites
- Well-Tempered Clavier

Alternatives:

- Telemann
- Handel
- Scarlatti
- Purcell
- Rameau
- Bartók Mikrokosmos

## Japanese, anime, and game music

Source hierarchy:

1. official score
2. licensed commercial arrangement, such as Yamaha or Shinko Music
3. reputable arrangement with named arranger and visible preview
4. MuseScore or community transcription, verified against a recording
5. YouTube, aural and interpretive reference only, never primary

Use IMSLP for public-domain repertoire.
Modern Japanese music requires licensed sources.

## Apps and tools

- **Piano Marvel** — note & rhythm accuracy, mic or MIDI. *Limitation: ignores dynamics/tone/articulation/pedaling; mic mode iOS-only, weak on dense polyphony.*
- **Anki + FSRS** — rotation & maintenance reminders. *Not validated for procedural decay — treat as heuristic.*
- **Pro Metronome** — tempo work. *Use as a check tool, then turn it off; overuse flattens expression.*
- **Phone camera** — performance recording.
- **Functional Ear Trainer** — ear training, free.
- **MakeMusic Cloud** — alternative assessment. *Same accuracy-only limitation as Piano Marvel.*

### Neuroscience of practice (for understanding, not daily reading)

- Sleep consolidation: motor skills consolidate during slow-wave sleep. Practice before bed.
- Interleaving: mixing tasks improves long-term retention vs blocked practice.
- Retrieval practice: effortful recall (Rule of Almost) builds stronger memory than passive review.
- Mental practice: activates same motor cortex as physical practice. Use for consolidation.
- Dual-task training: builds cognitive capacity for sight-reading and performance.
