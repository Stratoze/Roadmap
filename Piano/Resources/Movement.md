# Movement — Modular Technique Library

> The universal technique layer beneath all repertoire.
> A piece is a **composition of movements**. Movements are reusable, trainable patterns
> with form, travel path, and joint roles. Learn the vocabulary once; apply it to anything —
> Bach to Liszt to Ravel to Animenz. Only the composition changes, never the alphabet.
>
> This file is the **canonical source** for technique.
> [Roadmap](Roadmap.md) gates stages on these archetypes.
> [Practice Protocols](Practice%20Protocols.md) diagnoses errors against them.
> [Progression](Progression.md) sequences them over years.

## Evidence convention
| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `[CONVERGED]` | Traditional pedagogy and biomechanics research agree. Safe default for ~99% of players. |
| `[STUDY]` | Supported by motion-capture, force, or EMG research. |
| `[SCHOOL: X]` | One pedagogical tradition's claim. Useful, but not independently converged. |
| `[HYPOTHESIS]` | Reasonable, not yet verified. Test it. Promote to `[VERIFIED — date]` when confirmed in your own playing. |

Traditional schools were refined over ~150 years of observation. Modern biomechanics
(motion capture, joint-force modeling, EMG) now explains *why* much of what survived works.
Where studies and tradition agree, trust the convergence. Where only tradition speaks,
keep it but tag it. Where your body disagrees with the default, **document the deviation**
in your Personal Calibration (below) — don't silently drift.

## How to use this file

### 1. Movement mapping (new piece)
During preview, before note-learning:
1. Mark the score into passages and **name the archetype** for each (M1, M4, M7...).
2. Mark the **seams** where one movement hands off to another. Errors live at seams.
3. Identify the hardest seam: that is your first Q-spot.
4. Log the map in the piece note's Movement Map table.

### 2. Movement diagnosis (errors)
When an error recurs, don't just retry. Classify it:
symptom → suspect archetype → check that archetype's landmines → fix form → retest.
Use the [Diagnostic Table](#movement-diagnostics).

### 3. Composition rules (how passages are built)
- **Chain rule:** complex passage = sequence of archetypes. Practice the transitions,
  not just the pieces.
- **Gait transition rule:** the movement organization at slow tempo is *not* the same
  as at fast tempo — like walking vs running. Somewhere between your slow tempo and
  target tempo the movement must reorganize. If you only practice slow, you never
  install the fast gait. See [Speed Work](Practice%20Protocols.md#speed-work).
- **Velocity rule:** speed = smaller amplitude + more proximal drive + earlier preparation.
  Never "faster fingers." `[CONVERGED]`
- **Seam rule:** the last note of movement A and first note of movement B are practiced
  as one unit, at reduced tempo.

## Core principles (defaults for ~99% of players)

| # | Principle | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | **Proximal-to-distal.** Arm/shoulder initiate and power; fingers articulate. Chords descend shoulder → elbow → wrist → finger in a cascade. | `[CONVERGED]` |
| P2 | **Dynamic arch.** Knuckles stay high, but curvature is a *variable*, not a frozen pose. Curve deepens for speed/control, flattens for spans — the arch structure itself never collapses. | `[CONVERGED]` |
| P3 | **Neutral wrist default.** Wrist is free and mobile, roughly aligned with the forearm most of the time. Brief, loaded deviations are fine; sustained ulnar/radial deviation under force is the injury pattern. | `[CONVERGED]` |
| P4 | **Rotation over stretch.** Alternation (trills, tremolos, Alberti, arpeggios) is driven by forearm rotation, not by stretching the hand open. | `[CONVERGED]` |
| P5 | **Prepare in the air.** Chord shapes are formed before landing; leaps pre-shape during flight. Arrival is placement, not construction. | `[CONVERGED]` |
| P6 | **Release is technique.** Tension is deposited by every group of notes and must be refunded between them. No release budget = no endurance. | `[SCHOOL: Taubman/Fink]`, consistent with EMG economy findings `[STUDY]` |
| P7 | **Minimum amplitude at speed.** As tempo rises, finger height and gesture size shrink; drive moves proximally. | `[STUDY]` |
| P8 | **Anticipation.** Non-striking fingers pre-position for the next note (co-articulation). Smooth legato is mostly invisible preparation. | `[STUDY]` |
| P9 | **Weight for volume, not force.** Louder = more arm mass delivered, not harder finger pressing. | `[CONVERGED]` |
| P10 | **The body is one chain.** Feet grounded, spine tall, breath continuous. The finger is the last link, not the first mover. | `[CONVERGED]` |

### What 99% of players should NOT do
- Freeze a single "correct" curve regardless of context. `[CONVERGED]`
- Lift fingers high for speed. High lift at high tempo is inefficient and tense. `[STUDY]`
- Twist the thumb under with a contorted wrist; the thumb passes via lateral arm travel + rotation. `[CONVERGED]`
- Stretch the hand open to reach arpeggio notes; rotate instead. `[CONVERGED]`
- Hammer octaves with a locked wrist; the wrist is a spring. `[CONVERGED]`
- Press into the keybed after the note speaks; the sound is made at the bottom — pressing adds only tension. `[CONVERGED]`

## Individual calibration
The defaults above fit ~99% of players. Your body may sit in the 1%.
**Deviations are allowed; undocumented drift is not.** Write your adjustments here and
copy relevant lines into piece notes.

| Factor | Typical adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| Small span (< octave comfortable) | Octaves/chords: roll slightly, redistribute notes between hands, use pedal to cover gaps. M7 octave frame: play at the fallboard edge, shallower reach. M3: prefer thumb-over to deep thumb-under. |
| Large hands | Beware collapse from over-relaxing; keep knuckle structure. Avoid cramming into narrow five-finger positions. |
| Hypermobile / double-jointed fingers | Extra MCP stabilization needed. Watch first-phalanx collapse and DIP hyperextension. Favor slightly shallower arch with firm knuckle; avoid extreme curled positions. |
| Stiff joints / low flexibility | Increase lateral arm travel (M3/M4) instead of demanding finger span. Longer warm-up. |
| Forearm length / bench distance | Affects in/out geometry at black keys and lateral alignment. Re-check bench distance when anything feels like reaching. |
| Injury history | Any archetype that reproduces symptoms → stop, diagnose form, consider teacher/health professional. See [Technique and Health](Practice%20Protocols.md#technique-and-health). |

**Personal Calibration Log** (update when a deviation is confirmed):
- (example) `2026-08-12 — M3: thumb-over works better than deep thumb-under for my thumb angle [VERIFIED]`
- (example) `2026-08-12 — M7: octave span at edge of fallboard, wrist stays higher [VERIFIED]`

## The Movement Library

### Family A — Keystrokes
#### M1 — Isolated keystroke (the finger stroke)
**Used for:** scales, passagework, articulated lines, anything one-note-at-a-time.
- **Form:** dynamic arch, knuckles high. Curvature deepens with tempo; may shallow for wide intervals. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** MCP (knuckle) is the primary driver of the vertical descent; PIP/DIP joints act as stabilizers/shock absorbers, moving slightly in opposition. The fingertip arrives with intent; after key-bottom, no continued pressing. `[STUDY]`
- **Joint roles:** MCP = fulcrum and driver. PIP/DIP = stabilizers. Wrist = neutral conduit, free. Arm = supports and transports.
- **Tempo behavior:** slow = visible finger articulation; fast = tiny amplitude, proximal drive, fingers staying close, high individuation between digits. `[STUDY]`
- **Schools:** Ortmann's curved finger stroke; Matthay's touch categories; Taubman's "walking hand."
- **Landmines:**
  1. Frozen curve regardless of context. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. High finger lift at high tempo. `[STUDY]`
  3. Collapsed knuckle (first phalanx buckling) — force leaks, tone thins. `[CONVERGED]`
  4. Pressing after key-bottom. `[CONVERGED]`
  5. Wrist dipping or rising as a substitute for knuckle action. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** slow five-finger with exaggerated knuckle initiation; dotted rhythms for evenness; one-hand "whisper" practice (pp, minimal motion); note-grouping (3+1, 2+2, 1+3).
- **Calibration:** hypermobile players watch first-phalanx collapse; small hands avoid over-curving into cramped positions.

#### M2 — Arm-weighted keystroke (singing tone)
**Used for:** melody lines, lyrical voicing, deep legato tone, chords-as-melody.
- **Form:** arch maintained but the *source* of the sound is arm mass, not finger effort. Shoulder and upper arm deliver weight through a stable finger. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** the whole arm descends as a unit into the key; the finger is the last link, firm but not rigid. Think "through the key," not "at the key."
- **Joint roles:** shoulder/upper arm = source. Wrist = flexible coupling (never locked). Finger = firm endpoint.
- **Tempo behavior:** largely tempo-independent; at speed, weight transfers laterally from note to note like a rolling wave.
- **Schools:** Matthay's weight transfer; Sandor's gravity playing; Taubman's arm support.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Collapsing the endpoint — weight without structure = dull thud. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. Stiffening the wrist to "carry" the weight — kills tone and tires fast. `[CONVERGED]`
  3. Confusing loud with pressed. Weight arrives then releases. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** arm-drop onto single notes from ~5 cm, feel the transfer, immediate release; sing the phrase while playing; play the melody alone with exaggerated weight, then re-add accompaniment.
- **Calibration:** light-built players get loudness from more freely released mass, never from force.

### Family B — Travel
#### M3 — Scale passage (thumb-under / thumb-over)
**Used for:** scales, scale-like passagework, any line that exceeds one hand position.
- **Form:** arch maintained throughout. The hand does not contort; the *arm* translates laterally and the thumb arrives via a small rotation, not a wrist twist. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** play toward the thumb's destination — the arm carries the hand there while fingers finish the current notes. Thumb-under at slow/moderate tempo; thumb-over (lateral shift, no tucking) at high speed or in small hands. `[SCHOOL: Taubman]` + `[HYPOTHESIS]` on exact crossover tempo.
- **Joint roles:** forearm = lateral transport + slight rotation. Thumb = arrives prepared, plays from its side tip. Wrist = level, neither dipping nor hiking during the pass.
- **Tempo behavior:** as speed rises, the tuck shrinks into a lateral shift; the motion becomes almost a glide.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Wrist twisting/bending to shove the thumb under. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. Elbow sticking out or pumping as a compensation. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  3. Accent on the thumb note — the seam becomes audible. Fix by playing through the seam. `[CONVERGED]`
  4. Late preparation: thumb moves after the preceding finger, not during. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** thumb-pass in slow motion watching for wrist neutrality; grouped rhythms across the pass; "silent thumb" drill (thumb plays ghost-quiet until the accent disappears); two-octave loop without stopping at the turn.
- **Calibration:** small hands/short thumbs → thumb-over earlier; long thumbs → shallow under is fine.

#### M4 — Arpeggio travel (rotation-assisted lateral motion)
**Used for:** arpeggios, broken chords, Alberti spans, wide flowing figures.
- **Form:** arch held; the hand *rotates* toward each next note instead of stretching for it. In/out adjustment (see M4 note below) moves the hand between white and black key rows. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** forearm rotation carries the hand diagonally along the arpeggio; fingers stay relatively quiet. Lateral arm translation covers long distances. Wrist draws a gentle arc, never a rigid line.
- **Joint roles:** forearm = rotation engine. Arm = lateral transport. Fingers = stable endpoints. Thumb = passes with the same logic as M3.
- **Tempo behavior:** slow = visible rotation; fast = rotation amplitude shrinks to a shimmer, arm leads.
- **Schools:** Taubman in/out + rotation; Matthay rotation.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Stretching the hand open to reach the next note. `[CONVERGED]` — the #1 arpeggio error and a top injury pattern.
  2. Flat hand position on black keys; instead move in (toward fallboard) for black keys, out for white. `[SCHOOL: Taubman]`
  3. Wrist locked in a straight rail — it must arc. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** slow arpeggio naming "rotate" on each note; blocked practice (play the arpeggio as held chords, then break it); in/out awareness: play black-key passages deliberately closer to the fallboard.
- **Calibration:** short-fingered players rotate more and travel less; never substitute span for rotation.

#### M5 — Leap
**Used for:** displaced chords, jumps, rapid positional changes (Liszt, Ragtime, Chopin, Animenz).
- **Form:** the destination shape is formed **during flight**, not on arrival. Hand travels as one prepared unit. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** efficient arc — low and fast for small leaps, higher for large. The arm moves the hand; fingers do not reach ahead of the hand. Arrival = placement of an already-formed shape.
- **Joint roles:** arm = transport. Hand = pre-shaped. Eyes = optional; proprioception does the targeting once trained. Wrist = neutral on landing.
- **Tempo behavior:** more tempo → lower arc + earlier pre-shape. Never a later or bigger gesture.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Building the shape after landing (arrival = construction). `[CONVERGED]`
  2. Visual dependence: staring at the target breaks tempo and posture. Train eyes-free. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  3. Tensing during flight; tension must live only in the landing instant. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  4. Overshooting from a too-large arc. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** eyes-closed landings; "ghost leaps" (travel the arc without playing, feel the distance); stop-and-land: leap, land silently, verify shape, then add sound; reduce the arc progressively.
- **Calibration:** small hands pre-shape the exact top note first and let the lower notes roll if needed.

### Family C — Simultaneities
#### M6 — Chord grasp
**Used for:** homophonic chords, chorales, big Romantic textures.
- **Form:** the hand adopts a firm arch matching the chord's exact geometry *before* descent. Different chord = different mold. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** proximal-to-distal cascade on descent — arm leads, wrist arrives, fingers last. One motion, many notes, one sound. `[STUDY]`
- **Joint roles:** arm = mass. Wrist = stable-but-living fulcrum. Fingers = molded endpoints; each fingertip has a job.
- **Tempo behavior:** fast chord passages = molds prepared one ahead (anticipation), shallow descent between chords.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Shape assembled on arrival. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. All-fingers-equal when voicing is required → see M11.
  3. Stiff wrist between repeated chords (no refund) → fatigue. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Drills:** grasp-and-hover (form the chord above the keys, hold shape, drop as a unit); voice one note of the chord louder (M11); block-practice of broken textures.
- **Calibration:** spans at your limit → redistribute or roll; document in piece note, don't fight anatomy.

#### M7 — Octave frame and wrist spring
**Used for:** octave passages, octave chords, bravura textures (Liszt, Chopin polonaises).
- **Form:** 1–5 frame locked at the span; wrist stays springy — it is a suspension, not a hammer arm. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** hand bounces from the wrist; the arm supplies mass for loudness, the wrist supplies rhythm and release. Between octaves the frame travels, the wrist breathes.
- **Joint roles:** wrist = spring. Forearm = steady carrier. Thumb/5 = frame endpoints, side-of-finger contact on white keys.
- **Tempo behavior:** slow = full wrist bounce; fast = tiny bounce, frame held with minimal grip, drive stays in the wrist not the shoulder.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Locked wrist hammering from the arm — the classic octave injury. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. Death grip on the frame; grip only what the span requires. `[CONVERGED]`
  3. Thumb collapsing inward, shrinking the span mid-passage. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** wrist-only octaves at pp; bounce-stops (play, freeze, verify zero forearm tension); slow loud octaves with full release between each.
- **Calibration:** small hands play at the fallboard edge, wrist slightly higher; never force the span.

### Family D — Oscillation / Repetition
#### M8 — Rotational oscillation (trills, tremolos, Alberti)
**Used for:** trills, tremolos, Alberti bass, repeated alternating figures.
- **Form:** hand relaxed over the notes; the forearm rotates like turning a key — two notes per full rotation cycle. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** rotation axis through the forearm; fingers ride the rotation with minimal independent action. At very slow tempo finger stroke (M1) is fine; above a certain tempo, rotation must take over — a **gait transition**.
- **Joint roles:** forearm = engine. Fingers = passive riders, shaped but quiet. Wrist = free.
- **Tempo behavior:** the defining archetype of the gait-transition rule. Finger-based trills cap out; rotation-based trills scale.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Trying to make a finger trill faster instead of switching to rotation. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. Elbow pinned to the body, strangling rotation. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  3. Accenting one side of the rotation (uneven tremolo). `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** slow exaggerated rotation ("doorknob"); trill on black keys (2–3, 3–4) to feel the mechanism; tremolo with dynamic swelling while keeping rotation constant.

#### M9 — Repeated notes
**Used for:** repeated-note figures (Scarborough, Liszt, Prokofiev toccatas).
- **Form:** finger substitution where the tempo allows (2–3–4–1...), rotation-assisted where it doesn't; key surface contact kept shallow. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** fingers strike from close range; the wrist adds a micro-bounce at speed so the same tissue isn't hammered.
- **Joint roles:** fingers = strikers (rotated roster). Wrist = micro-bounce. Forearm = light rotation assist.
- **Landmines:**
  1. One-finger hammering. `[CONVERGED]`
  2. Pressing to the keybed between repetitions (blocks the rebound). `[CONVERGED]`
  3. Substitution chosen randomly instead of fixed. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** substitution ladders at slow tempo; crescendo on one repeated note without tension; rhythm-grouped repetitions.

### Family E — Control / Color
#### M10 — Co-articulated legato
**Used for:** seamless melodic lines, polyphonic joining, anything "singing."
- **Form:** relaxed arch; the hand is already organizing the *next* note while the current one sounds. `[STUDY]`
- **Travel path:** overlap and hand-off between fingers; non-striking fingers pre-position (anticipation). The connection is manufactured by preparation, not by stretching to hold notes. `[STUDY]`
- **Landmines:**
  1. Finger-pedal legato (stretching to hold everything) instead of hand-off + pedal. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  2. No overlap → audible gap; too much overlap → smear. Calibrate by ear. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** two-note overlaps practiced in isolation; sing through the line while playing; "one finger late" drill to feel the hand-off.

#### M11 — Voicing and weight distribution
**Used for:** melody-over-accompaniment, chord voicing, polyphony, any texture with a hierarchy.
- **Form:** same chord shape as M6, but arm weight is *steered* into the voiced finger(s). The voiced finger gets the weight; the others ride along with minimal mass. `[CONVERGED]`
- **Travel path:** asymmetric weight delivery within one gesture.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Playing everything equal "until later" — equal is a habit, and habits win. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  2. Achieving voicing by stiffening the unvoiced fingers instead of reducing their weight. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** play a chord with only the melody note sounding, then add the rest ghost-quiet; melody alone → melody + silent accompaniment → full; exaggerate voicing 3:1, then scale back.

#### M12 — Pedal footwork
**Used for:** legato pedaling, half-pedal, flutter, color.
- **Form:** heel grounded, ball of foot on the pedal; motion from the ankle, quiet and efficient.
- **Travel path:** legato pedal = down AFTER the new notes sound (syncopized), up-clean-down between harmonies. Half-pedal = partial reset that keeps bass bloom while clearing mud. Flutter = rapid shallow oscillation for dense textures at speed.
- **Landmines:**
  1. Pedaling from the whole leg (noisy, slow). `[HYPOTHESIS]`
  2. Pedal as a finger-legato substitute (blur hiding sloppy hands). `[CONVERGED]`
  3. Late lift → harmonic smear at cadences. `[HYPOTHESIS]`
- **Drills:** silent pedal changes (lift-clean-down without sound); half-pedal ladder (full → half → quarter → full); pedal the left hand alone while the right plays dry.

## Movement diagnostics
**Symptom → suspect archetype → first check.**

| Symptom | Suspect | First check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uneven passagework | M1, M3 | Knuckle stability; thumb seam accent |
| Clean slow, collapses fast | gait transition (any) | Are you still using the slow gait? Add speed work |
| Arpeggios feel like stretching | M4 | Rotation vs stretch; in/out position |
| Scale thumb accent or wrist twist | M3 | Lateral transport; thumb timing |
| Leap misses / inconsistent | M5 | Pre-shape in flight; eyes-closed drill |
| Octaves tire quickly | M7 | Wrist spring vs arm hammer; grip force |
| Trill too slow | M8 | Finger trill → rotation gait transition |
| Repeated notes fatigue | M9 | Substitution roster; no keybed pressing |
| Chords not together | M6 | Shape formed before descent |
| Melody doesn't sing | M2, M11 | Weight source; voicing drill |
| Legato has gaps or smears | M10 | Overlap calibration; anticipation |
| Sound muddy at speed | M12 | Pedal lifting fully between changes |
| Tension anywhere at speed | P6 release budget | Release between groups; shrink amplitude |

**Diagnostic protocol (2 minutes):**
1. Play the failing spot once, watching (or filming) the hands.
2. Name the archetype. Name the seam before it and after it.
3. Check the archetype's landmines in order. First hit = the fix to try.
4. Fix form at slow tempo, then retest at the tempo where it broke.
5. If form is clean and it still fails → it's a coordination/timing issue, go to
   [Learning Protocol](Practice%20Protocols.md#learning-protocol) chunking, not more form work.

## Sources and confidence
**Schools compared:**
| School | Core claim | What survived convergence | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Matthay | Forearm rotation + weight as basis of touch | Rotation for alternation; weight transfer; "invisible" arm work | Dense terminology; some dated physiology |
| Taubman | Rotation, in/out, shaping, alignment to natural coordinates | In/out geography, lateral scale travel, alignment-first injury prevention | Best with a trained teacher |
| Ortmann | Kinematic analysis of the curved finger stroke | MCP-driven keystroke; fingertip stabilization | Early 20th-c. methodology |
| Fink | Rhythmic grouping and gesture-based practice | Group practice, release points | |
| Sandor | Gravity and arm weight | Weight playing without force | |

**Modern research directions supporting the convergence:**
- Motion-capture keystroke studies: MCP-dominant descent; PIP/DIP stabilize in slight opposition; efficient joint coordination predicts accuracy and survives tempo increases. `[STUDY]`
- Finger-curvature force studies: more curved positions reduce MCP reaction forces → less joint/tendon stress. `[STUDY]`
- EMG economy studies: experts show less co-contraction, use gravity/relaxation on the upstroke, and co-articulate (anticipatory positioning). `[STUDY]`
- Injury epidemiology: sustained extreme wrist deviation + forceful stretch over-represented in playing-related disorders; small-handed pianists at elevated risk when forcing spans. `[STUDY]`
