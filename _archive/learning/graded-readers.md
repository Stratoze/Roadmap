# Graded Readers — absolute beginner

Set 2026-09-29. The learner's own words: keep these sources recorded here, and
point the learner at them when it is time to read, rather than making the
learner hunt for material.

**Tracking: manual, not automatic.** Graded readers are ~200-400 words each, so
a per-sentence log would cost more than it is worth. The tutor asks how many
were read and records that count. Everything else about them is automatic.

## Why graded readers

Measured 2026-09-29 over a 100 GB light-novel collection: **every** book sat at
0.11-0.31 known content vocabulary — best of 6,388 was 0.26 — including titles
an independent source grades "Beginner". The learner has ~1,500 words in Anki
(Kaishi 1.5k, frequency-ordered). Any narrative has 3,000+ unique words, so
coverage caps near 20% regardless of tier. That collection was deleted; the
material lives on the web, and it was never going to be readable this year.

Graded readers invert that. Two independent publishers publish explicit
vocabulary budgets and they agree:

| Source | Starter | Beginner |
|---|---|---|
| [NPO Tadoku](https://tadoku.org/japanese/en/levels-en/) | **200 words** | 350 |
| [JGR Sakura](https://jgrpg-sakura.com/library/) | A = **400** | B = 550 |

At 200-400 unique words the learner's 1,500-word deck covers the text almost
entirely. **The bottleneck is syntax, not vocabulary** — which is exactly the
current frontier (`jp-yokubi-00`, sentence anatomy and word order).

Both figures are industry-published, not peer-reviewed. They are the best
available evidence, and the two bodies agree, but they are not academic.

## The list, in order

### 1. JF いろどり (IRODORI) — grammar, first
<https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/>

Free, official Japan Foundation. 入門 (18 lessons) / 初級1 / 初級2 / 初中級.
PDF per level, **MP3 audio for every lesson**, plus ことばリスト and
文法ワークシート with audio. Furigana throughout.

Why first: the only free official material found that targets *sentence anatomy
and word order* directly rather than assuming it. **Start at 入門**, below
初級1.

### 2. JF Sydney Hiragana Mini Books — reading from zero
<https://classroomresources.sydney.jpf.go.jp/resources/hiragana-mini-books/>

Free, official. ~49 books across series; each introduces **one hiragana
character** in an 8-page short story. Flip book + printable PDF.

Why: the closest thing to "learn to read Japanese from nothing", and it sits
below kanji entirely.

### 3. Tadoku Free Books — short stories, graded and free
<https://tadoku.org/japanese/free-books/>
Level Start filter: <https://tadoku.org/japanese/book-search?level=l-start>

Free, furigana, PDF + audio + YouTube. Level Start = 26 titles at 200 words
and 0-200 words per story.

Verified Level Start titles: [犬と肉](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/40182/)
(Aesop; audio + YouTube; 155 reviews), [あっ、まるい](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/39479/),
[赤い・・・](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/8644/), [じゃんけん](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/8597/),
[どっち？](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/8137/), [どうぞどうも](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/6812/),
[TKG](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/6459/), [ちょっと来て！](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/61273/),
[何を飲みますか？](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/6447/), [アリとハト](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/9080/),
[しりとり](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/9078/), [一つ二つ三つ](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/9076/),
[絵の中のねこ](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/9079/), [わかがえりの水](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/9083/),
[森のコンサート](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/9084/), [金の斧銀の斧](https://tadoku.org/japanese/book/64329/).

Useful filter: `?attr=010` = ひらがなのみ (hiragana only) — the true-beginner shelf.

### 4. さくら多読ラボ — measurable progression
<https://jgrpg-sakura.com/library/>

Free with registration. 8 levels A-H by **published vocabulary count** (400 →
4,500), audio for every book, colour-coded. Level **A** is the entry point.

Why here specifically: it is the one source that publishes per-level vocabulary
counts, so progression can be *measured* against a fixed yardstick rather than
guessed. Also publishes retold classics (蜘蛛の糸, 羽衣物語) rewritten to
beginner vocabulary — the "first full story" shape.

### 5. 有名人 — the "finish a book" milestone (paid)
[にほんごよむよむ文庫 スタート](https://ask-books.com/book-details/?slug=9784866394701)

¥2,530 (paper) / ¥2,024 (ebook). **12 pages, 8 stories, hiragana + katakana
only**, all kanji furigana'd. Audio on Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and a free ZIP.
Level Start, NPO supervised.

Publisher's stated design intent, verbatim: *"Each booklet contains one complete
story, allowing learners to finish a book in one sitting, providing a sense of
accomplishment and boosting confidence."* (Marketing copy, but it is the
explicit rationale.)

Also: [Taishukan にほんご多読ブックス](https://www.taishukan.co.jp/item/nihongo_tadoku/)
¥2,400+tax, Level 0 includes 田舎のネズミと町のネズミ. Free level table PDF:
<https://www.taishukan.co.jp/item/nihongo_tadoku/pdf/level_only.pdf>

### 6. OJAD — grammar lookup, whole way through
<https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/>

Free, University of Tokyo. ~9,000 nouns, 42,300 accent patterns with male+female
audio. **Browsable by beginner-textbook chapter** (みんなの日本語 初級 第2版
lessons 1-50, まるごと 入門A1), which is a difficulty ordering. The
**動詞の後続語検索** shows which particles and forms can follow a verb — directly
serves "sentence anatomy, word order".

Caveat: a reference tool for words in context, not connected stories.

## Corrections to earlier advice

- **NHK Easy Japanese is no longer free.** It sits behind an NHK ONE account /
  broadcast-contract gate. `easyjapanese.eoc.nhk.or.jp` and `school.nhk.or.jp`
  are dead domains. Ungated equivalents with ruby:
  [西日本新聞 やさしい日本語](https://www.nishinippon.co.jp/theme/easy_japanese/),
  [WITH NEWS](https://withnews.jp/articles/series/60/1) (weekly, Saturdays).
- **Satori Reader is intermediate, not beginner.** Its own site says so three
  times. It is the only verified kanji/kana/furigana *toggle*, so it is worth
  remembering as a UX reference, but it is not material for this learner yet.
- Seven resources that sounded plausible do not exist: UniRead, インカム文庫,
  柜中物語, 几十万シリーズ, JREAD, 読売新聞の識字能力PRS, and the ja.wikisource
  titles 走上瀧宮 and 雨と十二の丸. Do not cite them.

## How the tutor uses this

When it is reading time, name the source and the level, and ask how many were
read. That is the whole procedure.

**Do not build a tracker, a player, a log, or a dashboard for this.** The
learner's words, 2026-09-29: *"just tell me to read and immerse, ill read and
immerse, done, no need for a dedicated tracker, that's laziness dressed up as
productivity."* Graded readers are a few hundred words each; the point is
reading them, not accounting for them. A count in conversation is the record.

The video library (D:\immersion, 1,718 videos) is a different case. Point the
learner at `complete-beginner` / `beginner` — the folder names are the level
labels, and a one-off measurement on 2026-09-29 put those folders at 0.4-0.7
known content vocabulary against the learner's deck, so they are the right
level. The 100 GB novel collection is gone and was a stretch tier regardless.
No tracking tool is needed for any of it.

### The one retention that earns its keep: video re-encode

The library was re-encoded to tier C (CRF 26, max 720p, preset fast, audio
96 kbps) into a new tree, because D: had only ~29 GB free against ~207 GB of
video. `scripts/encode_immersion.py` does it: read-only on the source,
resumable, verifies every output probes cleanly before it counts as done, and
copies the `.vtt` sidecars across (they are the only copy of the subtitles).
Result so far: **~52% of source size**, zero failures across 220 files.

This is a *disk* result, not a tracking one, which is why it survives the
no-machinery rule. Run it with `python3 scripts/encode_immersion.py`; it is
safe to re-run because it skips completed outputs.
