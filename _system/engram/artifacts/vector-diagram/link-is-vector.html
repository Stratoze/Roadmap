Dragging θ1 rotated link 1's arrow about the origin, swinging its head — and the whole chain attached to that head — to a new place. The arrows never changed length; only their directions changed. The chain stays head-to-tail, so the tip is always wherever the head of the last arrow lands, and the arrow from origin to tip is always the resultant.

Part 4 — Retrieve from nothing

Each of these is answered without the diagram. Write a first-draft answer, then reveal. This is how the idea becomes yours.

Retrieval 1. A 2-link arm has L1 = 3, L2 = 2, θ1 = 0°, θ2 = 90°. Draw it as a vector diagram: what are the two arrows, how are they arranged, and what single arrow is the tip position?

Link 1 is an arrow of length 3 pointing 0° from the world x-axis — straight right. Link 2 is an arrow of length 2 pointing 90° from the world x-axis — straight up — placed at the head of link 1. The two arrows are chained head-to-tail. The single arrow from the origin to the tip — the resultant — is the tip position; it lands at (3, 2).

Retrieval 2. No peeking at the diagram: which line is a joint angle measured from, and which way does the angle sweep? If a link sits at θ = 150°, describe where its arrow points.

From the world x-axis — the horizontal line through the joint, positive to the right — sweeping counterclockwise. 150° is a counterclockwise sweep past straight up, so the arrow points up and to the left, mostly up. (This is the rule that overrode the "point it however it feels" instinct: the angle, not the feel, decides.)

Retrieval 3. In the opening scene the tip came out to (3, 2). Where do the 3 and the 2 come from — how are they hiding inside the two arrows?

X is the sum of the arrows' horizontal runs: link 1 runs 3 right (3·cos 0°), link 2 runs 0 (2·cos 90°) → 3 + 0 = 3. Y is the sum of the vertical rises: 0 (3·sin 0°) plus 2 (2·sin 90°) → 2. Adding arrows head-to-tail is adding their components: the resultant's X and Y are exactly the summed components. Same frame picture as before — just chained.

Last move — the blank page. Close or cover this page entirely. On a blank sheet, reconstruct the whole argument from nothing:

  1. What is a link, as a vector?
  2. How are the two arrows of a 2-link arm arranged?
  3. What single arrow is the tip position, and how do its X and Y come out of the two arrows?

Write it down before you reveal. Then compare.

1 · A link is an arrow. Length L at joint angle θ = an arrow of magnitude L pointing θ, measured counterclockwise from the world x-axis.

2 · An arm is a chain. Link 1 starts at the origin; link 2 starts at the head of link 1 — two arrows head-to-tail, each pointing at its own θ from the world x-axis.

3 · The tip is the resultant. The single arrow from the origin to the tip closes the chain; it is the sum, and it is the hand. Its components are the summed components: X = L1·cos θ1 + L2·cos θ2, Y = L1·sin θ1 + L2·sin θ2.
