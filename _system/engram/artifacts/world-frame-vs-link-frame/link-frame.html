Link frames: rulers bolted to moving links

This is your Phase-4 arm, in miniature: a shoulder (θ1) and an elbow (θ2). Every point on it can be measured with three rulers — the world frame bolted to the floor, a frame painted on link 1, and a frame painted on link 2. The question of this artifact: which ruler sees which point move? — builds on: point-in-frame · world-frame → next: transform-frame · fk-chain

00 · Commit before the arm wakes

The pen tip is welded to the forearm (link 2), at coordinates (0.300, 0.000) in the frame painted on the forearm. You rotate the elbow (θ2) by 45°.

In the frame painted on the forearm, the tip's coordinates will:

The answer: stay (0.300, 0.000) — unchanged. Held. The worked drive below will prove it in three turns, and will also show you the harder half: what the world frame sees.

02 · Worked drive — three turns, three predictions

I drive the arm; you predict before every turn. Commit, watch the arm move, then explain one line.

Turn A — predict. The shoulder motor turns 30° (θ1: 0° → 30°). Which entries of the table change?

Actual: only the world column changed.

elbow: (0.400, 0.000) → (0.346, 0.200) in world · tip: (0.700, 0.000) → (0.606, 0.350) in world. The link-1 column held (0.400, 0.000) and (0.700, 0.000). Why? The link-1 frame is bolted to link 1 — it rotated with the arm. Its coordinates measure distance from link 1 itself, and the arm's own parts never moved relative to it. The world frame is bolted to the floor, so it saw the swing.

One line: why did the link-1 column stay frozen while the whole arm swung?

The link-1 frame is bolted to link 1 and rotates with it. "Elbow in the link-1 frame" asks how far the elbow is from link 1 itself — and the elbow is part of link 1. Constant. The world frame is bolted to the floor, not the arm — it sees the swing.

Turn B — predict. Now the elbow motor turns to 45° (θ2: 0° → 45°). Which entries change this time?

Actual: the tip's world entry and the tip's link-1 entry changed; everything else held.

tip in link-2 frame: (0.300, 0.000) — frozen. The link-2 frame is bolted to link 2, and the tip is welded to link 2: same body, no relative motion. In the world: (0.606, 0.350) → (0.424, 0.490). In link-1: (0.700, 0.000) → (0.612, 0.212) — the tip is not welded to link 1, so link 1's ruler sees it swing.

This also settles your opening commitment (section 00): the tip stays at (0.300, 0.000) in its own frame — while the world frame, and even link 1's frame, watch it move.

One line: why does the tip's link-2 entry hold (0.300, 0.000) while its world entry changes?

The link-2 frame is bolted to link 2, and the pen tip is welded to link 2 — the frame rides with the body the point is part of, so the point is stationary in it. The world frame is bolted to the floor, not the arm, so relative to the floor the tip swings.

Turn C — predict. The elbow swings back: θ2 goes 45° → −30° (θ1 stays 30°). The tip's link-1 coordinates: what happens?

Actual: live — (0.612, 0.212) → (0.660, −0.150).

Same motion: the tip froze in link-2's frame and moved in link-1's. Frozen-ness is a property of the pair (point, frame): the tip is welded to link 2, not to link 1. A frame being "bolted to a real link" is not enough — it must be bolted to the same link the point is welded to. The elbow (welded to link 1) is frozen in link 1's frame; the tip (welded to link 2) is frozen in link 2's frame.

One line: why did the tip freeze in link-2's frame but move in link-1's frame, in the very same motion?

Frozen-ness belongs to the pair (point, frame), not to the point alone. The tip is welded to link 2 and the link-2 frame is bolted to link 2 — same body, frozen. The tip is not welded to link 1, so the link-1 ruler sees it swing.

Worked drive complete. The deep feature: a frame is rigidly attached to the body it is bolted to. Points welded to that body are stationary in it — everything else is live. The sliders are yours now; the table's markers (● frozen / ○ live) are switched on.

03 · Free run — your hands on the arm

Drag both sliders across their ranges. Watch which entries stay and which spin — and which column depends on which joint.

When you've turned the arm enough: one sentence — what makes a column frozen, and why is the tip's link-1 entry not frozen?

A column is frozen exactly when its frame is bolted to the same link that the row's point is welded to. Matching bodies freeze; mismatched bodies move. The tip is welded to link 2, not link 1 — so the link-1 column sees it live.

05 · Retrieval 1 — say it in your own words

Cover the arm and the table. Why does the elbow keep coordinates (0.400, 0.000) in the frame painted on link 1, no matter how θ1 and θ2 turn? (2–3 sentences. Write before revealing.)

The link-1 frame is bolted to link 1 and rotates with it; the elbow is welded to link 1. "Coordinates in the link-1 frame" means distance from link 1, measured along the frame painted on it — the elbow is part of link 1, so that distance never changes. The world frame is bolted to the floor instead of the arm, so world coordinates record the swing.

06 · Four contrast cases — one deep feature

For each (point, frame) pair under a joint turn: does the coordinate change or stay? Commit all four, then reveal the rule. "Watch it" drives the arm to that pose — read the table as it moves.

Case 1. The elbow — measured in the world frame. The shoulder turns 30°.

Case 2. The elbow — measured in the link-1 frame. The shoulder turns 30°.

Case 3. The pen tip — measured in the link-2 frame. The elbow turns 45°.

Case 4. The pen tip — measured in the world frame. The elbow turns 45°.

Case 1 — changes. (0.400, 0.000) → (0.346, 0.200). The elbow is welded to link 1; the world frame is bolted to the floor — different bodies, so the elbow moves in world coordinates.

Case 2 — stays. The elbow is welded to link 1, and the link-1 frame is bolted to link 1 — same body: (0.400, 0.000) forever.

Case 3 — stays. The pen tip is welded to link 2, and the link-2 frame is bolted to link 2 — same body: (0.300, 0.000) forever.

Case 4 — changes. Same tip, but the world frame is bolted to the floor: (0.700, 0.000) → (0.612, 0.212).

The one rule: a point's coordinates in a frame change exactly when the point's body moves relative to the frame's body. A frame bolted to a link never separates from it — so points welded to that link are frozen in it, and live in every frame bolted to something else (the world included).

And because the rulers differ, the answers honestly differ: there is no absolute "where". Every coordinate answers "relative to which frame?" — the arm just gave you two true answers to the same question.

07 · The sensor question

A sensor is welded to the end of the forearm at coordinates (0.30, 0.00) in the frame painted on that link (the link-2 frame). The joint at the link's base — the elbow — then rotates 45°. What happens to the sensor's coordinates in the link's own frame, and what happens to its coordinates in the world frame? Explain why the two answers differ.

In the link-2 frame: unchanged — still (0.30, 0.00). The frame is bolted to the forearm and rotates with it; the sensor is welded to the forearm; the sensor-vs-forearm distance never changes. In the world frame: changed. The world frame is bolted to the floor, not the arm — rotating the elbow swings the forearm (and the sensor) through space. Same physical point, two rulers: one bolted to the moving body, one bolted to the floor.
