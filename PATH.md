# Path to the goal

A map only, like `Mechatronics/ROADMAP.md`. The study queues serve this
page; this page serves the goal: software engineering bachelor (~2027) →
master's in Japan → work in robots → permanent residence →
naturalization.

**North star (learner, 2026-11-03):** build a body for an AI to use,
within 5-10 years; not necessarily humanoid, shaped by the task. That
makes the field robot learning (embodied AI): hardware (actuators,
sensors, control) plus the software that lets a model drive it. Cars,
rockets and satellites stay only as job backups.

Written by Opus 2026-11-02 from web search results, not from the official
pages (they are blocked from the cloud session). Every fact marked
*unverified* must be checked on the official page before anything is

## The routes

- **A. Master's, October 2027, English-taught program.** Needs: an English
  score (IELTS 8.0 is strong; U Tokyo's School of Engineering sets no school-wide
  minimum, departments do; scores valid 2 years, from the T-cens page in
  search results), transcripts, a research plan, recommendation letters,
  and usually a professor who agrees first. Osaka's English robotics
  guide (2023) asks applicants to choose one supervisor. Funding: the
  university-recommended MEXT route, JASSO, university grants, or self.
- **B. Research student (研究生) first, master's exam after.** Slower, but
  gives a year in the lab and time for Japanese. The Embassy MEXT route
  fits here (apply April-June 2027 for 2028 entry).
- **C. Backup: work first.** The Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/
  International Services visa needs a bachelor's related to the job; a
  software engineering degree for a software job is the clear case
  (consultancy sources, *unverified*). A master's can come later, or not.

Pick one main route and one backup once the open questions are answered.

## One life (how Opus would run it, 2026-11-03)

The learner asked: plan as for an only successor with one life, who
varies day to day, not as a student to tutor. These rules come first;
the routes and queues serve them.

1. **No ruin.** Guard what cannot be undone before anything else:
   - Health: sleep and Vovinam first (fitness is tracked outside the repo).
   - Debt: never pay a broker or study agency; apply direct to
     universities and employers. Vietnamese trainees arrived with about
     674,000 yen of debt on average (2022 report, via Viet Nam News).
   - Visa status: never work past the legal limit (28 h a week for
     students).
   - The bachelor's degree: graduate on time; nothing here outranks it.
2. **Parallel, not one after another.** Apply on route A and route C at
   the same time and take the better offer. Applications are cheap; a lost
   year is not.
3. **A vs C, honestly.** A gives a lab, the hardware side the software
   degree lacks, company recruiting through the lab, and 10 more HSP
   points (master's 20, bachelor 10), and two years to reach N2 before the
   job (learner's point). It costs about two years of fees and
   living. C earns from day one and starts the residence clock, but the
   English-only robotics roles found (Mujin, Astroscale) mostly ask for
   experience; C most likely means a software job first, robots later.
   Decided 11-03: parents fund it, so A is main (B as its entry if
   timing needs it), C the backup.
4. **Strength first.** The learner is a software engineer, and the hard
   part of a body for an AI today is the learning: a model that turns
   camera images into motor commands. So enter through software (Python,
   then robot learning), and build the mechatronics under it: the
   capstone, then a learned arm (below).
5. **People before plans.** One talk with someone who did this (Vietnam
   software degree → Japan master's or job) beats a month of reading. Find
   two by 30 Nov: school alumni, LinkedIn, the university's international
   office. Ask: what they wish they had done a year earlier, what it cost,
   and who decided their admission or hire.
6. **Dated decisions.** Each decision has a date and a trigger (Deadlines
   table), so a bad month changes the route on purpose, not by drift.
7. **The lab is the learner's choice.** Contacts can handle housing and
   paperwork; admission is the professor and the exam, and the lab decides
   the two years, the network and the research plan. Choose it by the north
   star. If a helper charges fees, rule 1 applies. Hardware is the
   learner's own (3D printer, circuits; machining through local shops,
   found by networking), and so is compute (see the learned arm). What
   only a lab gives: the supervisor, peers, and the degree.
8. **Startup: job first, startup beside it, startup alone later.** The
   risk is not volatility but the visa: before permanent residence, the
   right to stay hangs on the job or school. Order: degree; a stable job at
   a robotics company (hardware at scale on its money); a side project
   within the visa (work outside an engineer visa's scope needs permission,
   資格外活動許可, and the employer's written OK); the startup alone once
   residence no longer hangs on an employer. Ideas can be tried in the lab
   as research first.
9. **Fallback without ruin.** No offer at graduation: work a year (in
   Vietnam at a Japan-linked company, or remote), keep Japanese going, and
   reapply. A language school only if chosen and paid directly.

## After arrival

- **Permanent residence:** the Highly Skilled Professional points system
  shortens the wait: 70 points → 3 years, 80 → 1 year. JLPT N1 gives 15
  points, N2 10 (from the official leaflet, as quoted in search results).
  The degree, age and salary points come from the official table: check
  it.
- **Naturalization:** the law still says 5 years, but since April 2026 the
  Ministry of Justice screens for about 10 years of residence
  (several secondary sources, *unverified*). Japanese is checked in
  writing and in an interview; no fixed JLPT level.
- **Japanese beyond N4:** the master's is also the Japanese buffer
  (learner, 11-03): two years in Japan before the job. Milestones: N4 July
  2027, N3 December 2027, N2 December 2028 (job hunting starts about a
  year before graduation), N1 after (HSP +15). The risk: an English
  program and English lab-mates let Japanese stall. Keep the daily track,
  and pick a lab with Japanese students and a part-time job in Japanese
  (within the student limit).

## After the capstone: a learned arm

The bridge from the capstone to a robot-learning lab, and the research
plan's first page: build an SO-101 arm (open source, about $100-300 in
parts; Hugging Face LeRobot), teleoperate it with its leader arm, record
about 50 demonstrations of one task, and train an imitation policy (ACT).
LeRobot first, ROS 2 later. Sized with the torque items already in the
queue (a joint motor must beat the arm's weight torque).

Compute: rent before buying. LeRobot has Colab notebooks; ACT trains in
about 1.5 h on an A100 (official notebook) or about 1 h on an RTX 3060
using about 2 GB of memory (one community test). Google AI Pro reportedly
includes Colab compute (unconfirmed). Buy a machine only when renting
costs more per month than owning and it runs most days; a single GPU
workstation comes before any server rack.

## What a lab will look for (and the gaps)

The capstone shows building and measuring. A robotics lab also asks for:

- **Linear algebra:** vectors, matrices, rotation matrices, the Jacobian.
  Not in any queue yet.
- **Programming for robots:** Python simulation, C/C++ on a
  microcontroller, Linux, Git, later ROS 2. The learner's degree is the
  strength here; the queues barely use it.
- **Control:** a loop that holds a position (PID). The passive arm has
  none; it is the natural next project.
- **Reading papers:** one paper from each target lab, summarized in plain
  words, before emailing the professor.
- **A research plan:** 1-2 pages that link the learner's work to the lab's.

Opus keys these once the target labs are known, so the queues aim at what
those labs actually ask for.
