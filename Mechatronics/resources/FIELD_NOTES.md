# Field Notes

> Glanced at during 12-week reviews. Not daily, not weekly.

---

## The actual test

> If I ended up in a room with someone who's spent a career on this, could I ask a question sharp enough that THEY learn something, or follow their answer well enough to push back?

The Hypothesis Loop is how you train for this. Every prediction you make and test is a rep. After 18 months, you show up with a falsifiable model, not just accumulated facts.

---

## Passive exposure, no quota — keep the tap open

- Follow 3–5 engineers/researchers in your domain. Read/watch one thing of theirs a month. Not for content — for calibration.
- Skim one open-source project's issue tracker a month: ODrive, SimpleFOC, VESC. See what real engineers argue about.

## Honest constraint map

| Target-adjacent environment | Known hard gate | What actually helps |
|---|---|---|
| NASA, civil service | US citizenship, often a degree | Depth + public work open the conversation; the gate is separate. |
| SpaceX | Degree strongly preferred; project-heavy bar | Hands-on projects carry real weight here specifically. |
| NIST | Degree typical, research-oriented | Depth + a publishable writeup matters more than volume. |
| Toyota / Tokyo Electron | Country-dependent; Japan skews university-pipeline | Systems thinking + shown work differentiates outside that pipeline. |

If a door requires a credential, that's a fact to know, not a reason to redesign the roadmap. Skill first.

## Look out for, radar not to-do

Open-source contribution opportunities · local robotics/makerspace groups · competitions, Formula SAE, CubeSat, rocketry · talks · internships · communities where people you respect hang out.

When one appears, evaluate against the current phase. Take it if it fits. Otherwise note it here and move on.
