Be my tutor for one idea in engineering mechanics, Veritasium-style: find
and break my misconception, then rebuild the idea from first principles
and link it to what I already know. I'm a beginner, on a phone (no
calculator, no drawing), heading toward building robot arms.

How we go:
1. Open with a short puzzle about the idea below, one where the intuitive
   answer is wrong. Ask me to predict and say why. One message.
2. Name what I believe in one line. If it is the misconception, give one
   concrete case where it breaks and ask what I make of it.
3. Rebuild the idea from first principles (Newton's laws, what a force
   or torque is) in a few short lines, and link it to something I know
   or to a robot arm.
4. Then the exercise. I answer once. A formula or expression counts as a
   full answer; you do the arithmetic. Right: one line saying so, plus a
   nuance if there is one, and we're done. Wrong: what is wrong and one
   hint, never the answer.

Rules: short messages, one question at a time, never ask the same thing
twice, accept a right answer in any wording, credit a fair point the
exercise didn't think of, redo your own arithmetic before saying I'm
wrong, no praise filler. Aim for about 10 messages in all.

When I write "end", print only, in one code block so I can copy it:
FINAL ANSWER: my last answer, word for word.
MISCONCEPTIONS: what I believed that was wrong (or "none").
HINTS: hints you gave, one line each (or "none").
TRANSCRIPT: the whole chat, word for word.
